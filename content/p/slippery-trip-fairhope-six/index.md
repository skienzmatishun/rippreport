## The 2010 BP Boom Contract Conspiracy

In May 2010, Mayor Timothy Kant and Senator Lee "Trip" Pittman, with the assistance of the Fairhope City Staff, conspired to deceive their constituents in their role of giving of the oil spill boom contract through the Fairhope Public Works Department.

At the time, Baldwin County staff informed Senator Pittman that an ethic complaint would be filed against Senator Pittman if he proceeded to seek the contract for his company, Pittman Tractor. The same day Senator Pittman submitted a replacement bid stating Oil Recovery of Mobile, Alabama, who then, with approval from Leigh Ann Ryals, Baldwin County Emergency Management Director, became the official contractor of record.

It is important to note that Trip's able accomplice, Tim Kant, Mayor of Fairhope, never told the public or city council of these events.

Two weeks later Mayor Kant announced that Fairhope would bid out the contract, "To make it more legitimate."

Strangely, Oil Recovery of Mobile, Alabama did not bid on the project. But, why should they have? They were, after all, already the official contractor of record!

Senator Pittman, a real "trip," on approximately May 11, 2010, went to Bay Minette Emergency Management Director Leigh Ann Ryals and demanded all paperwork related to the boom contract that he had previously submitted be returned to him. Also, it is important to note that at the time, Pittman Tractor did not have the required general contractor's license.

The good senator also visited the State Emergency Management Office in Clanton, Alabama, where thereafter, official contract documents went missing. Fortunately, unknown to Senator Pittman, Leigh Ann Ryals had previously filed the boom-related paperwork electronically.

On June 17, 2010, the boom contract was signed. It went to the low bidder—Pittman Tractor, for $625,575.00.

Prior to the contract signature date, on approximately June 12, 2010, Pittman Tractor was paid over $300,000 by the City of Fairhope toward the same unsigned contract.

The Baldwin County Commission and Fairhope City Council, never knew until after Fairhope's contract with Senoter Pittman was signed and annouced in the newspaper, what Trip Pittman and Tim Kant had done.
